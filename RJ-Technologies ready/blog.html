Connectivity

Choosing the right internet connection for your organization

A good connection starts with understanding how your organization works. List the tasks that depend on the internet, how many people use them at the same time, and whether those tasks involve video calls, cloud applications or large file transfers. This gives you a more useful starting point than choosing a plan by speed alone.

Next, check what connection types and service levels are actually available at your location. Ask about expected performance at busy times, installation requirements, support hours and how faults are handled. If your work depends on staying online, discuss backup connectivity and how the team will switch over if the main service is interrupted.

Finally, review the full cost and contract terms, including equipment, installation and any usage limits. A provider should be able to explain the options clearly and help match the service to your needs as they grow.

Cybersecurity

Everyday steps to make your business more secure

Strong security is built from consistent habits. Give each person a separate account, use long and unique passwords, and turn on multi-factor authentication wherever it is available. Limit access to the information and systems each person needs for their work, and remove access promptly when roles change.

Keep operating systems, applications and network equipment updated, and use reputable security software. Back up important information regularly and keep a protected copy separate from the devices used every day. Test that you can restore files; a backup is only useful if it can be recovered when needed.

Make it easy for staff to report unusual messages or activity without blame. Agree on who will respond, how to isolate an affected device and where to get help. A clear response plan can reduce confusion and help your organization recover more quickly.

Cloud technology

Is your organization ready to move work to the cloud?

Cloud services can make tools and files easier to access across locations, but moving without preparation can create new problems. Start by identifying the work you want to improve, the applications and data involved, and who needs access. Confirm that your internet connection can support those workflows reliably.

Before choosing a service, understand its costs, access controls, data handling, support arrangements and recovery options. Decide who manages user accounts and permissions, and document how your organization will retrieve its data if it changes providers or a service becomes unavailable.

Consider a small, low-risk pilot with a few users before moving essential work. Gather feedback, check that backups and permissions work as expected, and provide simple guidance to the team. A measured rollout helps you learn what needs attention before more people depend on the new setup.
